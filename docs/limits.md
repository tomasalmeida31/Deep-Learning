# Limits

Two lists. The first is claims that were checked and that the evidence does **not** support. The second is facts any honest write-up has to disclose. Each item gives the fact and where to check it. How to word them is up to the writer.

## A. Checked and not supported

| Tempting claim | What the evidence shows | Check |
|---|---|---|
| "The student matches the 99-evaluation sampler" | It is worse in 9/9 cells: S1 − E99 = +0.88 to +2.21 pp shape | `numbers.md` §1 |
| "The student beats tuned samplers up to 8 or 16 evaluations" | It beats the validation-selected sampler at up to 4 evaluations (9/9) and loses at 8 (0/9). Against the selected 6-evaluation sampler the difference is +0.07 pp, CI −0.49 to +0.64, 4/9 wins | `numbers.md` §1 |
| "The student is as good as (equivalent to) a tuned 6-evaluation sampler" | No equivalence test was run. A CI that includes zero only means the data cannot tell which is better. This CI (−0.49 to +0.64 pp) is wider than the 0.25 pp margin used for the gate, so equivalence within that margin is not shown | `numbers.md` §1, `method.md` §7 |
| "The student beats every few-step teacher setting" | It beats the setting that scored best on validation, out of a finite search: 196 + 63 + 9 settings; no 3-evaluation Euler or DPM-Solver++; σ_min fixed; no other solver families | `method.md` §5 |
| "The teacher needs 39 evaluations to match E99" | Only on the week-1 gate grid (the authors' noise schedule, plus a small sweep on seed 0). With the tuned samplers the teacher is within tolerance of E99 at 6–16 evaluations (adult 16/16/8, default 16/16/16, news 6/16/8) | `numbers.md` §3 |
| "The student is 99× (or 4×, 6×) faster" / "the student saves a lot of time" | Measured on one A40, a whole table takes 0.08–0.30 s with the student and 2.6–3.7 s with E99: 12–38 times as long, not 99, because decoding costs the same for every method and is 55–86 % of the student's time. Against the tuned 6-evaluation teacher (no detectable quality difference) the gain is 1.5–2.8 times, 0.12–0.16 s per table. The student's 6–12 minutes of training pay back after 3.5–7.1 million generated rows against E99 and 73.5–150.8 million against F6 | `numbers.md` §12 |
| "About 6 NFE" as an estimate | F6 was measured directly; quote the measured comparison | `numbers.md` §1 |
| "Downstream utility is unchanged" | AUROC is lower than E99 by 0.003–0.004, with CIs below zero; news R² (log scale) is lower by 0.011–0.020. No detectable AUROC difference from F8 (CI −0.0050 to +0.0013), but that is not equivalence, and there is no log1p-target F8 utility on news | `numbers.md` §5 |
| "The remaining gap is something one evaluation *cannot* recover" | One frozen recipe and one parametrisation were evaluated on all cells. 19 recipe variants were tried on adult s0 only, and training length only at 20k and 40k. Nothing shows that a different recipe or more training could not close the gap | `method.md` §4, `numbers.md` §8 |
| "The error is in heavy-tailed columns" | The five news columns with the largest excess on the common marginal metric have zero 99th-percentile error, and their mass beyond the real 99th percentile rounds to 0.000 (below 0.0005), for student and E99 alike; they are bounded or spiky few-valued columns. On default's heavy-tailed PAY_AMT columns, the tail errors are the same size for student and E99. Tail error is not zero everywhere (e.g. kw_min_max, num_videos). These two tail measures do not point to tails for the columns checked, but they do not capture every aspect of tail shape, so they do not rule tails out | `numbers.md` §7c |
| "The error is in pairs involving categorical columns" | That contrast appears only in SDMetrics' breakdown, which scores numerical-numerical pairs with a different metric from the other pairs. On one common metric there is no consistent pair-type effect, and the largest excess is default's numerical-numerical pairs. So the contrast depends on the choice of metric; why the two metrics disagree was not studied | `numbers.md` §7b |
| "After IPF, what remains is the relationship error" / "most pair error is caused by per-column error" | IPF is a sensitivity check: each two-column table is refitted separately, 50 sweeps, no convergence check. It does not split the error into causes | `numbers.md` §7b, `method.md` §6 |
| "The student fails where the coarse teacher fails" | Spearman ρ is 0.27 / 0.01 / −0.18, below the 0.5 threshold set in the ablation plan | `numbers.md` §7a |
| "Categorical columns are worse" (or numerical) | The type gap reaches 0.5 pp on 1 table only; the rule in the ablation plan needs 2 | `numbers.md` §7a |
| "CT cannot converge" / "the teacher step is necessary" | Teacher-initialised CT is worse than CD at 20k updates in 9/9 cells. Continuing it to 40k updates on seed 0 of each table did not narrow the gap: CT 40k − CD = +6.08 / +5.63 / +5.03 pp at 1 / 2 / 4 evaluations, and CT 40k − CT 20k = +0.14 / +0.20 / +0.07; its held-out loss (the CT loss) changed by at most 0.12 % over the last 2,000 updates. Supported: with this recipe and up to 40k updates, replacing the teacher step makes the student much worse. Not tested: CT with a recipe tuned for it (e.g. the full improved-CT recipe), CT from scratch, or longer training. The automatic READING line in `results/ablation_report_all.txt` ("the teacher's ODE step is necessary") is the output of a rule from the ablation plan and states as general what was shown for one recipe | `numbers.md` §8 |
| "N = 40 is worse" | N = 40 was still improving at 20k steps | `numbers.md` §8 |
| "More steps of the student always help" | S4 − S2 = −0.02 pp (5/9). On news s0, 2 and 4 evaluations are worse than 1. The 2-evaluation τ search chose its smallest candidate (0.5) in 6 of 9 cells, so S2 and S4 may be limited by that grid | `numbers.md` §1 |
| "No generator copies training rows" | DCR measures closeness, and exact matches were only checked against a random subsample of train' the size of the test set | `numbers.md` §6 |
| "The floor is the best possible score" | The floor is the average error of 5 bootstrap resamples of the training data, a reference for metric noise, not a lower bound | `numbers.md` §3 |
| "First few-step distillation for tabular data" | Khungurn et al., "Distilling Two-Timed Flow Models by Separately Matching Initial and Terminal Velocities" (arXiv 2505.01169, May 2025, preprint), distil flow-matching teachers and report 1/2/4/8 evaluations on the numerical UCI density tables GAS, HEPMASS, MINIBOONE and POWER (checked by the independent audit). A narrower "first" claim, e.g. first consistency distillation of a mixed-type latent tabular diffusion model, would need a full literature search, which was not done. **Read this paper before citing it** | — |
| "Every rule was fixed before the runs" (as a checkable fact) | That order is the authors' account: the dated protocol and ablation plan are not in this repo, so it cannot be checked from here. One amendment is recorded (the E5 determinism rule) | `method.md` §8 |
| Comparing our numbers with the TabSyn paper's tables | Not like for like: 10 % smaller training set, validation carve instead of test in the VAE, re-implemented MLE and DCR, and E99 run with churn 0 | `method.md` §1–2, §6 |

## B. Must be disclosed

| Fact | Detail | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | 3 tables (2 classification, 1 regression), 3 training seeds each, one student per teacher (variation between student training runs not measured). The 9 cells are not 9 independent datasets | `method.md` §2–3, §7 |
| Statistics | p-values are per comparison and not adjusted for the number of comparisons. At table level, the same sign on all 3 tables gives p = 0.25 | `method.md` §7 |
| Recipe tuned on evaluated cells | 19 recipe variants on adult s0 and 1 check on adult s1, both among the 9 evaluated cells. Without them the main ordering is unchanged | `method.md` §4, `numbers.md` §1 |
| Unequal search effort | Teacher: 196 + 63 + 9 sampler configurations per cell. Student: 1 / 7 / 13 τ schedules. The teacher search is still finite | `method.md` §5 |
| Our 1-evaluation teacher | The authors' code cannot sample in 1 step; D(σ_max·z, σ_max) is our definition | `method.md` §5 |
| Churn | E99 is run with churn 0. The authors ship churn 1; the difference is +0.007 pp on average (range −0.04 to +0.08) | `numbers.md` §1 |
| Student parametrisation | Differs from the canonical c_out scaling of Song et al. | `method.md` §4 |
| CT ablation | Teacher-initialised; only the target changes. The iCT-style arm adds two of the improved-CT changes, not the full recipe. The 40k CT follow-up covers 3 cells (seed 0) and is descriptive only | `method.md` §4, `numbers.md` §8 |
| Latent size | The diffusion model and student work on 60 / 96 / 192 numbers (the CLS token is dropped). `review_summary.json` R7 includes the CLS token | `method.md` §2 |
| Teacher seed spread | E99 on adult ranges from 0.76 to 2.34 pp across training seeds, a spread of 1.58 pp, about the same size as the student–E99 gaps (0.88–2.21 pp). The paired design compares each student with its own teacher | `numbers.md` §2 |
| Default trend excluded | Floor too noisy (sd 2.38 pp). Excluded from reported tests and the gate; still used as a tie-breaker in selection | `method.md` §7 |
| VAE leak in the original code | The authors' VAE schedule reads the test loss; we used a validation carve. The authors' setup was re-run for one seed per table only | `method.md` §2, `numbers.md` §11 |
| news validation carve | 37 rows were moved from validation to train' because their target value was absent from train'. This changes the training data and the train' scoring reference as well as validation (val p99 31,665 → 24,170). Student and teacher share it | `results/news_split_audit.json` |
| MLE and DCR re-implemented | MLE differs from the authors' script in feature transform, grid (4 vs 36 settings), selection metric, no refit on all rows, a log1p target on news, and XGBoost on CPU ('hist') instead of GPU ('gpu_hist'). The DCR reference is a train' subsample of test size. Same protocol for every generator | `method.md` §6 |
| DCR | No shift in closeness to training rows was detected; exact matches checked against a subsample only; not a privacy guarantee | `numbers.md` §6 |
| Unequal sampling seeds | E99's C2ST uses 10 seeds per cell against 20 for the student and the F_k; adult s0's E99 utility uses 20 seeds against 10 for the student | `method.md` §3 |
| C2ST split noise | SDMetrics' C2ST split was unseeded in the original runs; seeded re-scoring gives the same comparisons | `numbers.md` §4 |
| Reproducibility | For one student file and one E99 file per cell (sampling seed 0), the regenerated shape score matched the stored score exactly in 7/9 cells. default s0 and s1 differ by 0.052 and 0.039 pp (student) and 0.004 pp (teacher, s1). Only scores were compared, not samples | `numbers.md` §9 |
| Regeneration on default s0 and s1 | The later re-scorings regenerated the samples. On these two cells the student files (and on s1 the E99 and 15-evaluation teacher files) did not regenerate exactly: up to about 0.2 pp per file, at most 0.022 pp on 20-seed means. §4, §6 and §7 use the regenerated samples there; the 0.1 pp stop rule was checked on the seed-0 pair only | `numbers.md` §9 |
| Recorded rule amendment | The E5 determinism rule fired at 0.052 pp and the re-run used "record, label, stop above 0.1 pp" | `method.md` §8 |
| news utility rule | news utility is R² on log1p(target); the rule for reporting it (real > E99 > 1-evaluation teacher) was checked on the final test scores | `method.md` §8 |
| Pair-metric code error | Synthetic values outside the real categories or bins were mishandled; fixed 11 Oct 2026. It never occurred in the scored files, so no number changes | `tools/eval_pairs_common.py` |
| 40k-step arms (CD and CT) | Continued from the 20k checkpoint with a fresh GPU random stream: a valid continuation, not bit-reproducible | `numbers.md` §8 |
| Speed benchmark scope | One A40, one batch of \|train'\| rows per table, median of 5 runs. Student training time comes from its run record; two of the nine runs were twice as fast for an unrecorded reason. The τ search, the teacher and the VAE are not counted, and neither is the F_k's own sampler search, so the break-even against F_k is stated in the baselines' favour | `numbers.md` §12 |
| Compute | TabSyn jobs: 60.4 GPU allocation hours (all job states) up to 2 Oct, plus 1.1 for the 11 Oct follow-ups; 23.8 for the study plus ablations | `numbers.md` §10 |
